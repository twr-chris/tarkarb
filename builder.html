Reading the recoil model — what these numbers actually mean

Tarkov shows four recoil numbers and most sources describe one of them wrongly. Here is what each does, taken from the game's own ShotEffector and confirmed by live-fire testing.

Vertical recoil
RecoilForceUp		 The size of the kick in the plane you are looking at. It drives the climb — and, steered by the recoil angle, every bit of sideways drift too. This is the number that decides whether you can hold a burst on target.
Horizontal recoil
RecoilForceBack		 The shove straight back into your shoulder, on the depth axis. Despite the name it is not left-right sway and it does not move your shots sideways at all. It is also on a different scale from vertical, so the two are not comparable to each other — compare vertical across guns, or horizontal across guns, never one against the other on the same gun. The wiki and most guides call this "side-to-side drift." That is incorrect.
Recoil angle
RecoilAngle		 Which way the kick points, in degrees. 90° is straight up. Below 90° the group walks right; above 90° it walks left. Fixed per weapon — no attachment changes it.
Dispersion
RecolDispersion		 Randomises the kick angle from shot to shot, spreading it into a cone. This — not horizontal recoil — is where lateral wander and the classic S-pattern come from. Also fixed per weapon.

Why this tool shows a single recoil number: every attachment carries one recoilModifier applied to both axes, so they always scale together and their ratio never changes. Verified against all 393 in-game presets, and visible on any weapon screen — a stock AK-101 reads 71 / 179 against the bare receiver's 98 / 248, which is ×0.7245 and ×0.7218: the same multiplier twice.

From ShotEffector, per SPT-Realism's recoil overhaul:

RecoilDirection = Vector3(-sin(θ) * vertRecoil,   // climb
                          cos(θ) * vertRecoil,   // lateral
                          hRecoil)               // back into the shoulder

θ = Random.Range(angle - dispersion, angle + dispersion)

Read that and the diagram follows: the on-screen kick is driven entirely by vertical recoil, aimed at the recoil angle, with dispersion jittering that aim each shot. Horizontal recoil sits on its own axis, outside the picture.

Confirmed live — 30-round auto dumps, aimed, no mouse input:
